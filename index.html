Six-second social video · Android first

Less scrolling. More irl’ing.

irl.ing is a social video app made of six raw seconds. Hold to record, let go to pause, and your moment loops for your friends. No filters, no bottomless feed. Watch, laugh, then go back to your actual life.

How it works

Notice it. Hold it. Loop it.

  1. 01

    Notice

    Something small. Something very you. A face, a place, a perfectly timed bad joke.

  2. 02

    Hold

    Hold to record, let go to pause, hold again. Six seconds, stitched from as many takes as you like. Comedy lives in the cuts.

  3. 03

    Loop

    Send it to your people. It plays on repeat on their screen, exactly as raw as you made it.

Then put the phone down.

The network we’re building

A social network with an ending.

Inspired by Vine, the six-second app a lot of us still miss, and rebuilt around one idea: the best thing your phone can do is show you something real, then let you go.

  • Six seconds, max.

    Long enough to say something. Short enough to get back to your day.

  • Raw only.

    No filters, no face-tuning, no beauty mode. What you saw is what your friends get.

  • Friends before followers.

    Loops from people you actually know come first. Everyone else can wait.

  • Feeds that end.

    When you’re caught up, we say so. No autoplay rabbit hole. Go outside.

Status Recording, loops and everything in the lexicon below work today in the loops lab. Profiles, friends and feeds are being built next, Android first.

Every loop has an entrance

Loops don’t load. They arrive.

The lexicon

New words for a tiny new thing.

Under the jokes and the sunsets is some serious engineering. We gave it names you can say out loud.

Hold-to-Loop
Press to live it, let go to pause it, press again. Six seconds from as many takes as you like.
One continuous 30 fps clip, stitched on recorded time.
Featherweight
A whole loop in about 100 KB. Light enough to send on one bar of signal.
AV1, encoded on your own phone, up to two passes to land on size.
Lifeboats
Loops travel in two-second lifeboats. If one sinks, the rest still float.
Independent chunks, each with a checksum and a sync marker.
Lighten Up
Busy network? A relay drops the in-between frames and passes it on: half the weight, still whole.
Two temporal layers, trimmed without decoding or re-signing.
Real Seal
Every loop is sealed by your phone. Change one frame and the seal tells on you.
Ed25519 over every chunk’s hash, with a key that can’t leave the device.
True Grain
We measure the grain your camera actually saw and put it back when the loop plays. Labeled, never faked.
Parametric film grain, after AV1’s film-grain synthesis.
Glow-Up
Tiny files, rebuilt crisp on the phone that plays them.
Game-engine upscaling and sharpening (AMD FSR 1), plus dither.
Smart Pixels
The entrance every loop makes: pixels that find their way home.
Oriented splats sampled from each frame, leaning along its edges.
Mars Manners
Important parts first, damage kept small, everything checked on arrival.
Habits borrowed from how rovers send pictures home.
