How it works

Three steps. Then go live your life.

  1. 01

    Notice

    Something small. Something very you. A face, a place, a perfectly timed bad joke.

  2. 02

    Hold

    Hold to record, let go to pause. Six seconds, stitched from as many takes as you like. Nothing to adjust, nothing to fake.

  3. 03

    Share

    Your loop is squeezed to about 100 KB on your phone, signed with a key that never leaves it, and rebuilt lifelike when it plays.

Then put the phone down.

Tiny on purpose

Built for bad signal. And far-away places.

We borrowed habits from the people who send pictures home from Mars: send the important part first, keep damage small, and verify what arrives.

Smart pixels

Every loop finds its way to you.

Loops arrive as a scatter of pixels that fly home, lean along the edges of what you saw, and settle into the real thing. Slide to stay somewhere in between.

Under the hood: game-engine upscaling and sharpening (AMD FSR 1), the grain your camera actually had, and a touch of dither. All of it happens on the phone that plays the loop.

Android first

The loops lab is open.

It works today in Chrome on Android: record, squeeze, sign and share a loop as a file. Friends, feeds that end, and links that open anywhere are next.
